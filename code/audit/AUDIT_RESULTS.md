## Part B: HadCET (genuine real data), claim "weather-to-climate persistence crossover near ~10 days"
Data: Met Office HadCET daily mean, 93,096 days 1772-01-01..2026-11-20, no missing days, sha256 da61cbb4...; deseasonalised on 1772-1850; DFA-2, 15 scales 7..896 d; P1 = 1851-1950, P2 = 1951-2025.
**Verdict (pre-registered rule, Amendment 1 margin 0.25): SUPPORTED, with caveats. The original margin 0.10 gives the same verdict but cannot discriminate (null drift 0.17).**
| period | E4c declared decline (margin 0.25) | E4c point slope H(7 d) -> H(~100 d) | E4c contrast point range | E6 location set (best) / step | E6 misfit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 1851-1950 | yes | 1.41 -> 0.73 | -0.84 .. -0.11 (lowest bound -1.44) | 2.6-20.1 d (10.1 d) / -1.17 | ok |
| P2 1951-2025 | yes | 1.50 -> 0.69 | -1.22 .. -0.16 (lowest bound -1.79) | 2.6-20.1 d (2.6 d) / -2.91 | ALARM |
Reading, in order of importance:
1. The decline is real and replicates out of period: in both periods the honest E4c band excludes any change smaller than 0.25 and the equivalence test (all contrast intervals inside +-0.25) fails; relative to a fGn(H = 0.65) reference (no crossover) the excess slope is +0.58 at 7 d, +0.39 at 20 d, +0.26 at 40 d, +0.17 at 56 d, +0.09 at 112 d and about 0 beyond ~150 d. So the weather scale carries extra short-memory structure that fades by ~60-110 d, and long scales sit at H about 0.65-0.75.
2. It is a gradual relaxation (like an AR(1)-type memory) rather than a sharp step at 10 d. E6 places a logistic step at 10.1 d in P1 (matching the claim) but its location set is open at the low end (2.6 d is the edge of the search grid), so the data bound the step location only from above (< 20 d); in P2 the best fit sits at the grid edge and E6's own misfit alarm fires. The location part of the verdict is therefore weak: "supported" means a decline with a step located below ~20 d, not that 10 d is pinned.
3. Pipeline calibration on synthetic records of the same layout (100 y, 100 replicates): no false change at margin 0.25 for any estimator (0/100, CP upper 3.6%); E4c covers the population slope 93.0% (E6 48%, E1q 28%, E0 0%), so E4c is the only band whose coverage statement can be trusted on DFA data. On a known crossover (AR(1)+fGn, population contrast 0.56) E4c declares at margin 0.25 in 81% of records (coverage 93.9%), the others in 100% with 0% coverage. At margin 0.10 even pure fGn triggers declarations (E4c 20%, E6 24%, E1q 34%) because DFA-2 itself bends by 0.17: this is why the primary margin was moved.
4. **Is the record long enough? (recipe).** The recipe's law (J = 15, an extrapolation beyond its fitted J 7-11) predicts half-widths 0.51 (E4c) / 0.22 (E6), but measured are 0.14 / 0.06: about 3.6x narrower, so the J-law is far too pessimistic for a 36,500-day series. Using the measured half-width: the observed change S_obs = 0.84 (P1) / 1.22 (P2) for E4c passes the recipe's detect test (ratio 4.3 / 6.6 vs threshold 1.9), so the records are long enough for what they show; the literature-size change S = 0.30 would NOT be certifiable at margin 0.25 (ratio 0.36 vs 1.9; the law says about 115 window points for E4c, 31 for E6). The observed change is about 3x the literature-size one, which is what makes the declaration possible.
Limits: one record; year-cluster bootstrap understates dependence beyond ~1 y and the AnDi results suggest bands may under-cover at long scales (E4c's synthetic coverage 93% is the check); last scales (>600 d) are noisy (P2 E4c slope falls to 0.27 at 896 d); the literature numbers (S_claim = 0.30, direction) are from memory and were not re-verified; E6's recipe constants were fitted on walker data, not DFA.
Figures: `figB1_cet_dfa.png` (DFA curve, local slope with E4c/E6 bands, E6 location sets), `figB2_cet_synthetic_calibration.png`.

## Bottom line
AnDi (simulated, labelled): only the bias-aware E4c keeps near-nominal coverage (94-95% at T <= 150, 90% at T = 200); no estimator has power on 200-frame switching tracks, and the recipe says about 1,500-3,000 frames (E0/E6) or more than 20,000 (E1q/E4c) would be needed. HadCET: crossover claim supported (decline of 0.6-0.9 in local DFA slope, replicated in 1951-2025), location only bounded above, gradual not sharp, and the recipe's J-law is too pessimistic here by 3.6x in half-width.

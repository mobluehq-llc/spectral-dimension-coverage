\section{Benchmark details}\label{sup:bench}
\paragraph*{Parity ablation and independent recomputation of the truth.}
Laziness removes the parity zero of bipartite graphs ($P(t)=0$ at odd $t$ for the non-lazy walk), a textbook effect: in an ablation on two cells, all three estimators run (E0, E1, E2) abstained on every dataset when the time grid of the non-lazy walk contained odd times. On an all-even grid the non-lazy walk, scored against its own exact curve (which is not the lazy walk's curve at the same times), gave coverage comparable to the lazy walk's: for E0, 95.5\% against 95.0\% on the ring and 91.0\% against 95.5\% on the 2D torus. The truths were recomputed independently in SageMath in 120-bit arithmetic (maximum deviation $1.4\times10^{-13}$ in $d$) and the gasket spectrum was rebuilt from a separate construction (identical for generations 1--7).

\paragraph*{Constructions.}
All fits are in $u=\ln t$ with $y=\ln\hat P$. Local-polynomial fits use a Gaussian kernel in $u$ multiplied by the return counts as weights, with no boundary correction beyond the polynomial order; the bandwidth is chosen by leave-one-out cross-validation on half A, from $\{0.5,0.7,1,1.4,2\}$ for local-linear and $\{0.7,1,1.4,2,2.8,4\}$ for local-quadratic fits. Half A fixes the window, the bandwidth and (for E4c and E4r) the bound $M$; half B supplies the estimate and its bootstrap. The bootstrap resamples the 200 walker blocks ($B=200$ draws; 400 probe draws for E2, 80 for the collapse arms, 1000 for the E7 box) and re-runs the estimator on each draw; on deterministic graphs it has no graph level. A 95\% simultaneous band is $\hat d_j\pm c\,\mathrm{se}_j$ with $c$ the 0.95 bootstrap quantile of $\max_j|d^*_j-\hat d_j|/\mathrm{se}_j$ over the window. The change band is built in the same way for the contrasts $\Delta_j=d(t_j)-d(t_0)$, $j\ge1$, with its own maximum over $j$, so the search over tested times is inside the calibration. For E4 the weights $a^{(j)}$ at each window point minimise $1.96\sqrt{a^{\top}Sa}+(M/6)\sum_i|a_i||u_i-u_j|^3$ subject to annihilating constants and quadratics and reproducing the slope, with $S$ the bootstrap covariance of $y$ on half B; $M$ bounds $|d^3\ln q/du^3|$ on the window range; the band is $\hat\beta_j\pm(c\,\mathrm{sd}_j+B_j)$ with $B_j$ the worst-case bias term, and the change band uses $B_j+B_0$. E4c takes $M$ as the largest $|6\times\text{cubic coefficient}|$ of a local-quartic fit on half A over the window points; E4r takes the largest third derivative of one weighted quartic fitted to the whole window. For E6 the set of step locations and widths $(m,w)$ in a 0.99 confidence set from the profile residual sum of squares on half A (a grid of 21 by 6 values) is carried to half B, where for each member a 0.96 Wald ellipse for $(d_1,H)$ is formed; the band is the union of the resulting curves, at overall level 0.95 by the union bound. The plan and code in the archive give the remaining details.

\paragraph*{Further variants.}
Six variants were run in addition to the estimators defined in the main text: E2 with a Hoeffding band, a single-size collapse (E3s), E1q undersmoothed, E4o, and two D\"umbgen-type monotone bands \cite{dumbgen2003}, which failed the control bar at 92.4--92.6\%. The single-size collapse variant (88.8\% declarations on non-realised crossover cells) was not rescored in Amendment 10a and is outside any corrected conclusion.

\paragraph*{Details of the rescoring of Amendment 10a.}
For E0 and E1q the tested window is the estimator's own window on that dataset and the excursion is taken from the first grid time, $t_0=24$. For the collapse arms a declaration is the contrast between the exponents fitted to the late and the early half of the collapse range, and the tested window is the range of grid times of the largest of the three graphs; over it the median exact minimum excursion is $-1.24$ to $-2.30$ in the five cells. On the flattest cell alone the false-change counts are 1/199 (E3) and 0/196 (CL). Scoring the excursion between the centres of the two collapse regions instead gives the same counts.

Parts 9(b) and 9(c) are CONFIRMATORY; 9(b) passed and 9(c) failed its gate (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:rep}). The replication was itself defined only in the ninth amendment, after the results it tests were known.

Three further amendments followed review. Amendment 10 adopts three checks named by a referee, with the referee's decision criteria as bars: (a) a margin-consistent rescoring of change declarations on stored datasets, and (b) outer bounds for E7 and E7j, on datasets regenerated from seeds already analysed; both are therefore CORRECTIVE REANALYSES (Secs.~\ref{M-sec:collapse}, \ref{M-sec:e7}); and (c) a budget comparison at fixed windows, on fresh seeds, with a diagnosis of the E4 bound (CONFIRMATORY; Sec.~\ref{M-sec:fixed}). Table~\ref{M-tab:ledger} gives the outcome of each of these tests and of the two confirmatory parts of the ninth amendment. We distinguish two kinds of negative outcome. When an arm misses a bar, that is a measured result of the benchmark, whether or not we expected it; the failure of the regression-error arms in Amendment 12 was predicted before the run. When a statement that we made in advance does not hold, a prediction has failed. This happened for the second-design gate 9(c) and, among the exploratory amendments, for the transfer test of the sizing rule, the size bar (M) of E7 and E8 and the alarms for E6. The criterion of 10(a), which our earlier collapse claim had to meet in order to be kept, was not met. Three of the passes carry caveats, which we state where the results are reported and summarise in Sec.~\ref{M-sec:limits}.

A third round of review added no confirmatory run and changed no bar, seed or estimator. It added corrective reporting (a reconciliation of the E7 counts, an exact verification of a sample of linear programmes, complete tables for Amendment 10c, and native-target scores) and one EXPLORATORY diagnosis of the oracle E4 arm (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:fixed}), whose specification was committed before it was run.

A fourth round of review, by two referees (ChatGPT and Gemini), likewise added no confirmatory run and changed no bar, seed or estimator. Its four checks were specified in a file committed before they were run and are EXPLORATORY: the origin of the cross-environment drift of E7 (Sec.~\ref{sup:env}), the in-sample covariance of E4 (Sec.~\ref{sup:e4}), the reasons for fixed-window abstentions (Sec.~\ref{sup:10c}) and what the stored results support for the size of the joint test (Sec.~\ref{sup:e7}).

A fifth round added the joint-simulation-law check (Sec.~\ref{sup:env}), the studentised-error check (Sec.~\ref{sup:e4}) and the tables of Sec.~\ref{sup:native}. A sixth round (ChatGPT and Gemini; six external rounds in all, the sixth the last before this version) asked for reporting corrections only and added no simulation and changed no bar, seed or estimator: the scope of the Amendment 10b criterion (Sec.~\ref{sup:e7}), bracketing counts for every retained threshold (Sec.~\ref{sup:native}), the definition of the strata of the studentised-error check (Sec.~\ref{sup:e4}) and typesetting.

In all, fifteen estimators are scored in the exploratory benchmark, six further variants were run, seven arms were added in Amendment 12, and four misfit alarms were tried for E6; family-wise error over these looks is not controlled. Three further consequences should be weighed. First, the estimator set grew in response to earlier results: three bias-correction arms, three rules for $M$ and three shape-restricted bands were examined, and all are reported. Second, the premature rate was defined after the first run had shown the effect for E3; only the CL result was obtained after the metric was fixed. Third, the seventh amendment needed two corrections. Its plan misstated which estimators' windows shorten at 8 walkers per node; an erratum was committed before its predictions were computed or any cell was run. Its first scoring pass then contained two errors, found after the data had been seen: abstentions of E0 were dropped where the plan counts them as misses, and the 32-walker baseline was scored at sizes where E0 and E1q had never been run. Both were corrected, and the counts for the pre-specified exploratory bars were the same before and after.

\section{Computing environments and the cross-environment drift}\label{sup:env}
The runs were made in two environments, and the same seed does not give the same dataset in both. Amendments 5--8 and parts (a) and (b) of the ninth were run on one machine with its system Python. Its library versions were not recorded with the results; read in the third round they are NumPy 2.4.6 and SciPy 1.17.1. Part (c) of the ninth amendment and Amendments 10b, 10c, 11 and 12 were run on a second machine, the last four with NumPy 2.5.5, SciPy 1.18.1, pandas 3.1.1 and python-flint 0.9.0 (for part 9(c) the versions were not recorded). For the first run and Amendments 1--4 the environment was not recorded. So Tables~\ref{M-tab:wpn} and~\ref{tab:e7out}, the replication row of Table~\ref{M-tab:conf} and the entries of Table~\ref{M-tab:main} from Amendment 5 onwards (CL, M, Mb) come from the first environment (NumPy 2.4.6). The outer-bound reanalysis, Tables~\ref{M-tab:10c}, \ref{tab:e4o} and~\ref{M-tab:gk}, the other rows of Table~\ref{M-tab:conf} and the per-cell tables of Sec.~\ref{sup:10c} come from the second (NumPy 2.5.5). The walk simulation draws binomial counts from the generator, and in part of the datasets the same seed gives a different realisation: of the 1030 seeds that Amendment 10b regenerated, the window length equals that of the first environment in 752. The divergence is not universal. In one replication cell checked earlier, every output except those of E7 agreed to within $10^{-9}$ between the two. The cause was examined in the fourth round (next paragraph). The environments differ in NumPy version and in processor, and a third (an Apple arm64 machine with NumPy 2.5.5) gave a third set of window lengths on three test seeds, so the library version may not be the only factor. The consequence is that results from two environments on the same seeds are two Monte Carlo realisations and not one dataset scored twice (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:e7}). No gate compares per-dataset values across environments. The archive pins the second environment and regenerates every table from the stored per-dataset files; it does not re-simulate.

\emph{Fourth-round check of the E7 drift} (EXPLORATORY; specification committed before the run). In the reproducibility spot check of Sec.~\ref{sup:soft}, one replication cell re-run under NumPy 2.5.5 matched the stored results in every discrete outcome while 51 continuous E7 values differed, including a box constant of 3.5176 against a stored 3.5541. A referee asked whether an unseeded or state-dependent bootstrap could be responsible. It is not. (i) The E7 box and its bootstrap use only the generator passed to them, a child of a seed sequence fixed by the cell, dataset and estimator; the code contains no global random state, no unseeded generator and no dependence on set or dictionary order. (ii) On the cell of the spot check (ring $L=128$, 32 walkers per node, dataset 0) the box was computed twice in one process, in isolation, after reseeding the global generator with two different seeds, and after the other estimators had run: all results were bit-identical, as were the first five bootstrap resamples and the first five studentised maxima. (iii) The difference lies in the simulated walk. The integer return counts at the last three grid times are 45, 41 and 28 under NumPy 2.5.5 and 45, 36 and 34 under NumPy 2.4.6. Under 2.5.5 the last time has fewer than 30 returns and is handled by a Clopper--Pearson interval (15 times in the bootstrap part); under 2.4.6 it has 34 and joins the bootstrap max-$t$ (16 times), which raises the maximum. The bootstrap resamples are the same in the two versions, and the box constant is 3.5176 under 2.5.5 and 3.5541 under 2.4.6, the stored value. A prediction made beforehand, that a count within rounding of 30 flips under floating-point error, failed: no count is within $10^{-4}$ of 30. (iv) Re-running the whole spot-check cell (70 rows: 10 datasets, 7 estimators) in an environment identical to the pinned one except for NumPy 2.4.6 reproduced every non-timing value of the stored results bit for bit. The stored replication results are thus consistent with walks drawn under NumPy 2.4.6; this does not prove which version the first machine ran. (v) Scope, by sampling: for datasets 0--29 of every cell of the replication at 32 and at 8 walkers per node (960 datasets each), the walks differ between the two versions in 628 and 567 datasets, in 22 of 32 cells at each budget. In this sample the small cells never differ (2D torus $L=24$, 3D tori $L\le24$, 3D crossovers $L\le112$, 2D crossover $L=128$) and the larger cells mostly do; the first difference is usually in the last few grid times. As a crude proxy for the window-based estimators, the first differing time lies inside the exact window in 325 and 276 of the 960. The cells at 128 walkers per node and an exhaustive scan of all 200 datasets were not run.

This is a platform-dependent realisation of the walks and not a defect, so no reported number is wrong. It affects bit-level comparisons of E7, E7j and E8 across environments (these bands use every grid time and are the most exposed), the regeneration of Amendment 10b in the second environment (Table~\ref{tab:xwalk}), and any estimator whose window reaches a differing time. Whether amendments other than the replication at 32 walkers per node were run on walks of the NumPy 2.4.6 kind was not established.

\emph{Joint simulation law and a fifth-round check} (EXPLORATORY; no estimator changed). Each walker is simulated as a single path: its position is advanced through successive grid times and its return indicator is recorded at each (\texttt{p22lib.py}, \texttt{\_direct} for tori and products of cycles, \texttt{\_steps} for general graphs), and a block is the sum over its walkers. Counts of one block at different times are therefore sums over the same walkers and are correlated, as for the real walk, and not independent binomial draws. We checked this on the ring $C_{128}$ (lazy walk) at times 10, 24 and 34. For one walker the exact covariance of the return indicators at 24 and 34 is $q(24)[q(10)-q(34)]=0.009142$; the simulation gave $0.009132$ (standard error $0.000013$; 40 runs of $2\times10^6$ walkers), and the marginals agreed ($q(24)=0.11457$ against $0.11458$, $q(34)=0.09640$ against $0.09635$). For blocks of $m=20$ walkers (800\,000 blocks) the covariance was $0.1843$ (standard error about $0.002$) against the exact $m\cdot0.009142=0.1828$; independent draws would give zero (script \texttt{round5\_dep\_check.py}).

\section{History of the sizing rule}\label{sup:sizing}
EXPLORATORY.
Before this run we had fitted, on the 3D$\to$1D cells at 32 walkers per node, a sizing rule in the number $J$ of window points: a change of exact size $S$ is detected if $J\ge J_{\min}$ and $(S-0.25)/\mathrm{hw}(J)\ge T$, with half-width $\mathrm{hw}(J)=a(J/7)^{-\delta}$ and constants fitted per estimator. At that budget it placed the 2D minimum $L$ within one coarse grid step for seven estimators. Five of those answers were known when the rule was written; for E4c and E4r the prediction (768 for both) was committed before the cells were run, against a measured $>768$ (power 0.79) and 768 (0.92). The walker-budget run was the rule's pre-specified transfer test, and the rule failed it. With constants frozen, 7 of 16 predictions were within one fine-grid step (bar: 14). A variant with half-widths scaled as the inverse square root of the budget, also fixed in advance, scored 13 of 16 and failed too. By the rule set beforehand, the recipe is design-specific and not transferable. The half-widths show why: at fixed $J$ they depend on the budget (means on realised crossovers from 8 to 128 walkers per node: 0.39 to 0.095 for E0, 1.82 to 0.52 for E4c, 1.75 to 0.33 for E6), so the window length alone does not carry the precision. Section~\ref{sup:audit} shows that the half-width law also fails on a different data layout. We therefore offer no sizing rule. The sizes in Tables~\ref{M-tab:main} and~\ref{M-tab:wpn} are measurements in one design each.

\section{Structure-based bands E7, E8 and E7j}\label{sup:e7}
\subsection{Definitions and the Amendment 8 run (exploratory)}
The eighth amendment (EXPLORATORY) asked whether the trade-off survives when a band uses only what is guaranteed. For the lazy walk the spectrum of $S$ lies in $[0,1]$, so $q(t)=\int e^{-st}\,d\nu(s)$ with $\nu\ge0$ and an atom of mass $1/N$ at $s=0$: $q$ is completely monotone and $\ln q$ is convex. \textbf{E7} forms a simultaneous 97.5\% box for $q$ at the grid times (a block-bootstrap max-$t$ box where a time has at least 30 returns, exact CP limits with a Bonferroni split elsewhere) and reports, at each window time, the smallest and largest $d(t)$ over all such measures consistent with the box. The 2.5\% is allocated as follows in the code. The bootstrap part (1000 draws) takes the 0.99375 quantile of the largest absolute studentised deviation over its times, a nominal error of 0.625\%. The remaining times share 1.25\% equally, each with a two-sided CP interval. The nominal total is 1.875\%. The plan gave each part 1.25\%; the code is more conservative in the bootstrap part, and we left it unchanged. The set of times that receive a CP interval (those with fewer than 30 returns), and its size, are read from the same data; the Bonferroni division treats that set as fixed and makes no allowance for its selection. Given the box, the band is a deterministic function of it, so the nominal level of the band is that of the box; the bootstrap part is nominal and not exact. Each endpoint is a linear-fractional programme, solved as a linear programme on a measure discretised to 400 atoms log-spaced in $s$ plus atoms at $s=0$ and $s=\infty$; the atoms are doubled until no endpoint moves by more than 0.005. E7 has no bandwidth and no bound $M$, and it does not abstain by the window rule; it can, however, return no band when no measure fits the box. \textbf{E8} uses the convexity of $\ln q$: at each window time the lower limit is the largest of the secant expressions $2t[\ln\hat q(t)-\ln\hat q(t_+)]/(t_+-t)$ over the next two grid times $t_+$, and the upper limit is the smallest of the corresponding expressions over the previous two, each widened by $c$ standard errors with $c$ the 0.95 bootstrap max-$t$ quantile over all the secants used. With exact $q$ on this grid the E8 bracket is $[0.84d,1.18d]$, so E8 is wide by construction. The pre-specified exploratory bars were those of Sec.~\ref{M-sec:bench} and two more fixed in advance: (X) coverage of the exact curve on realised crossovers $\ge90\%$; and (M) a minimum $L$ at least one fine-grid step below that of E4c, i.e.\ $\le512$ in 2D and $\le160$ in 3D.

At 32 walkers per node both pass (C), (F) and (X). E7 covers 99.8\% (CP 99.6--99.9) on controls and 98.8\% (98.4--99.1) through realised crossovers; E8 covers 99.7\% and 99.8\%; neither made a false declaration in 2800 control datasets. These E7 figures have fixed denominators: a dataset with no band is a miss. Table~\ref{tab:e7out} gives every outcome. Of 9400 datasets, 66 have no band because the discretised problem is infeasible on the final atom grid. That shows only that no measure on the grid fits the box, not that no continuous measure does; counting these datasets as misses is the conservative choice for coverage. All 44 misses on realised crossovers and four of the five on controls are of this kind. In 170 datasets (1.8\%) feasibility changed when the atoms were doubled a second time; nine of these are among the 66, and the other 161 have a band that covers the exact curve and are counted as covered in the figures above. If all 170 are instead counted as misses, coverage is 2783/2800 $=99.4\%$ on controls and 3471/3600 $=96.4\%$ on realised crossovers, still above bars (C) and (X). With the exact $q$ in place of data, the E7 band contained the exact $d(t)$ in all seven cells tested. E7 is much narrower than E4c on small graphs (0.84 against 2.44 on the ring $L=128$). Both failed (M) under the change test of Sec.~\ref{M-sec:bench}, which compares band endpoints: E7 needed $L=256$ in 3D and had power 0.00 up to $L=768$ in 2D; E8 reached power 0.61 at $L=256$ in 3D and at most 0.01 in 2D. A fourth alarm for E6, disjointness of the E6 and E7 bands, fired on none of 1930 control datasets and on none of E6's 441 misses.

\subsection{The joint test E7j (exploratory)}
Comparing band endpoints is conservative, so part (a) of the ninth amendment specified in advance a joint test, E7j: the extremes of $d(t_j)-d(t_0)$ over the same set of measures, on the same data, with the same bars (F) and (M). E7j made no false declaration in 2800 control datasets. In 3D its power rises from 0.06 to 0.61 at $L=128$ and from 0.28 to 0.90 at $L=160$, so $L=160$ is the smallest tested size at which the discretised joint test reaches 0.8 (179/200), one step below the threshold of E4c (192) and level with E4r, with no tuning constant, and still above the 96 of E1q. Section~\ref{sup:e7jsize} states what the outer-bound recheck supports. In 2D the power stays at 0.00 at every size up to $L=768$. The largest drop the constraint set can certify there is about $-0.2$, short of the 0.25 margin, so this null does not come from the change test. By the pre-specified verdict E7j does not meet the bar (F passes, M fails). With a joint test the exact constraint set reaches a smaller size in 3D and detects nothing in 2D, although a real crossover is present there. Three qualifications apply. The joint test was specified after the Amendment-8 result on these data was known. The plan's bisection was implemented as an exact branch-and-bound (tolerance 0.002). Of the 7400 datasets, 48 had no feasible measure and so no declaration, and of the 740 datasets with a decision, all re-run at twice the atoms, one decision was withheld because the two runs disagreed; none of these is counted as a detection. E7j was run at 32 walkers per node only; with the endpoint test at 128, E7's 3D minimum is 80 (E4c: 112) and its 2D power is 0.72 at $L=768$. E7 and E8 rest on complete monotonicity, which mean-squared displacements and fluctuation functions lack, so they are not used in Sec.~\ref{sup:audit}.

\subsection{Outer bounds (Amendment 10b; corrective reanalysis)}
A band computed on a discretised measure can be too narrow, because the discretisation may exclude admissible continuous measures, and doubling the atoms is a diagnostic and not an enclosure. We therefore recomputed E7 and E7j with an outer relaxation. The support of the measure, $s\in[0,\infty]$, is partitioned into the atom at $s=0$, 1597 geometric cells that tile $(0,1]$, the first of which reaches down to $s=0$, and a tail cell $[1,\infty]$. For mass $m_k$ on cell $k$, $q(t_j)$ and the numerator of $d(t_j)$ are enclosed between the sums of $m_k$ times the minimum and the maximum over the cell of $e^{-st_j}$ and of $se^{-st_j}$. The tail is enclosed in the same way and is not truncated: it adds at most $e^{-t_j}$ ($4\times10^{-11}$ at $t=24$) to the upper enclosure of $q$ and nothing to the lower. The constants are computed in 80-bit ball arithmetic and rounded outward, and the box constraints are relaxed separately at each time, so the feasible set contains every admissible continuous measure. The upper endpoint is the smaller of two linear programmes and the lower endpoint the larger. Two relaxations were run, the plan's literal one (A) and this tighter one (B). \emph{Criterion and its disposition.} The plan's bar for 10b reads: ``coverage, controls, F, and the 3D min $L = 160$ result are preserved under certified bounds. Otherwise report the widened results as the corrected ones.'' The plan did not say which relaxation; at scoring we required E7 to meet (C), (X) and (F) under both A and B, and the joint test E7j to reach power 0.8 at $L=160$, 192 and 256 under B alone, because E7j was implemented only for B. The requirement that the 3D joint-test result be preserved under the plan's literal relaxation A is therefore \emph{unfulfilled}: E7j has no A implementation. The three powers below are B-only numerical results, and nothing across both relaxations is established. The reanalysis took 1030 dataset identifiers (cell and index, which fix the seed), chosen beforehand: the 170 flagged as atom-sensitive in Table~\ref{tab:e7out}, the one with the withheld decision, a seeded sample of 300 stable ones, and all 600 of the 3D cells $L=160$, 192 and 256 that set the minimum size (overlaps merged); and the seven exact-$q$ cells.

\emph{Two realisations of the same seeds} (third round; corrective reporting). The reanalysis did not rescore the datasets of Table~\ref{tab:e7out}. It regenerated them from their seeds in the second computing environment (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:bench}), in which part of the walks differ: the window length equals that of the first environment in 752 of the 1030 identifiers. \section{Exact verification of the linear programmes}\label{sup:lp}
Third round; corrective reporting on new computation; no bar. The programmes are solved by HiGHS in double precision, which leaves the solves themselves unverified. To check them, 44 of the 1030 datasets were selected by a rule and a seed committed before any was solved: all seven with a non-zero solver status in any column, eight random ones from each of the three 3D minimum-size cells, eight atom-sensitive ones, four stable ones and the one with the withheld decision. For each, every endpoint programme of relaxation B was rebuilt, 2016 in all, and solved by dual simplex, and the final basis was checked in exact rational arithmetic, with each double read as the rational number it is: primal feasibility of the basic solution and the sign of every reduced cost. All 2016 were verified to be optimal in this way: 1646 at the solver's own basis, and 370 after exact rational pivots from a basis that was dual infeasible by less than the solver's tolerance of $10^{-7}$. Fourteen datasets needed a second pass with a tighter tolerance and a higher pivot limit. The reported endpoints differ from the exact optima by at most $8.2\times10^{-8}$ in relative terms, and an interior-point solve agrees with the simplex solve to $2.9\times10^{-9}$. For the one dataset without an outer band, the emptiness of the relaxed set was certified by a strictly positive exact optimum of a phase-one programme. For the joint test we compared the decisions with this error. A drop is declared when the upper bound on the change lies below $-0.25$. Over the 570 declared drops the smallest margin is $2.4\times10^{-5}$; two are within $10^{-4}$ and eight within $10^{-3}$ of the threshold, against a solver error of about $4\times10^{-7}$ in absolute terms. The nearest undeclared bound is $2.7\times10^{-3}$ on the other side. If the four declarations at 3D $L=160$ that lie within $10^{-3}$ of the threshold were all lost, the power there would be 166/200 $=83.0\%$. Three things remain unverified. The exact check covers 44 of the 1030 datasets. It covers relaxation B and not A. And the 370\,259 programmes of the E7j branch-and-bound are covered by the margin analysis and not one by one. No one-sided certificate was produced for the joint-test declarations. We therefore describe the outer-bound results as verified on a sample and otherwise numerical.

\subsection{In-sample covariance (fourth round; exploratory)}
Specification committed before the run; no gate. In E4 the weights at each window point are optimised against the covariance $S$ of the 200 bootstrap draws of half B, and the critical value is then the 0.95 quantile of the studentised maximum of the weighted draws over the \emph{same} 200 replicates; the window, the bound $M$, $S$ and the weights are held fixed in that bootstrap. A referee suggested that this in-sample use produces optimism of the kind known from portfolio optimisation (Markowitz), in which an optimiser favours directions whose variance the estimated covariance understates. We tested it on the ring $L=512$ at 32 walkers per node with $M$ exact (no dataset has a bias above its allowance in any arm), in three variants: V0, the baseline; V1, 1000 bootstrap draws for both $S$ and the critical value, still from half B; V2, $S$ (and hence the weights and the standard deviations) from 200 bootstrap draws of the independent half A, with the critical value from the half-B draws. Each was run on the 200 original datasets, where V0 reproduced the stored rows exactly, and on 1000 fresh seeds, added because 200 datasets fix a coverage only to about three points.

\emph{Fifth-round check of the 0.86 ratio} (EXPLORATORY; no new simulation). A median bootstrap standard deviation divided by a marginal empirical one could differ from 1 merely because the bootstrap standard deviation varies from dataset to dataset (a scale mixture). We therefore computed, from the stored per-dataset values of the fresh-seed run (1000 datasets, 13\,653 pairs of $n_j$ and $\mathrm{sd}_j$), the standard deviation of the studentised stochastic errors $z_j=n_j/\mathrm{sd}_j$. It is 1.167 for the baseline, 1.118 for 1000 draws and 1.178 for the independent split (a stratum is a window length $J$ together with a window point $j$; $J=13$ has 359 datasets and 13 points and $J=14$ has 569 datasets and 14 points, giving 27 strata with at least 100 datasets, script \texttt{round6\_sdz\_strata.py}; median over strata 1.165, 1.124 and 1.166; strata range 1.052--1.283, 0.995--1.215 and 1.058--1.296 for the three variants), with a mean of $z_j$ within 0.05 of zero, and $|z_j|>1.96$ in 9.2, 8.1 and 9.7\% of pairs. A scale-mixture artefact would give about 1.00. The measured result is an excess spread of the studentised errors, about 1.17 against 1, close to $1/0.86$, in this cell and also with the independent covariance. Its magnitude alone does not establish that the conditional standard deviation is underestimated uniformly across window points or datasets, and we do not claim that; its cause remains unidentified.

\section{Causal sets (exploratory)}\label{sup:causet}
As a stress test without a known truth we ran lazy walks on undirected Hasse diagrams \cite{em2014} of sprinklings into flat $1{+}1$ and $3{+}1$ diamonds, $N=500$--8000 (100 sprinklings, 40 at $N=4000$, 12 and 9 at $N=8000$), with random geometric graphs of matched $N$ and mean degree in two and four dimensions as a null. The ensemble-mean curve has no plateau: it rises to a peak of 4.6--7.2 ($1{+}1$) and 6.2--10.9 ($3{+}1$) at $t\approx7$--14, above the manifold dimension, then falls as walkers reach the boundary. The peak grows with $N$ in the null as well (exponents 0.13--0.16 against 0.07--0.09 in $1{+}1$; 0.19--0.21 against 0.20--0.21 in $3{+}1$); the level differs (7.2 against 3.1, and 10.9 against 7.2, at $N=8000$). The estimators contradict one another: E1q reports a rise on all 73 non-abstained datasets, largely because it cannot follow the downturn at the window edge; E2 reports a drop on 10 datasets whose window passes the peak and a rise on 7 whose window does not; the sign of E0 changes with $N$ and grid; E3 sits at its search ceiling. At these sizes, which are small for this literature, these implementations did not deliver a consistent verdict on a flow of $d_s$. That is a statement about the implementations and windows used here, not about whether a flow can be measured. There is no known truth and the reference curve is post hoc, so this section is EXPLORATORY and supports no claim beyond that sentence. The non-local operator of Ref.~\cite{belenchia2016} is a different object from a walk on a graph, and we make no statement about it.

\section{Exploratory audit on labelled and real data}\label{sup:audit}
This section is EXPLORATORY and carries no headline claim. The plan for it was committed before any estimator was run on the data. E0, E1q, E4c and E6 are used unchanged; the input is $-\tfrac12\ln F$ against log scale, so that the estimated ``$d$'' is the local slope $d\ln F/d\ln s$. For tracks $F$ is the mean-squared displacement and the slope is the local exponent $\alpha$. For the temperature record $F(s)$ is the root-mean-square DFA fluctuation (an amplitude, with $F^2$ the mean squared residual over boxes), and the slope is the local DFA exponent.

\subsection{Labelled simulated tracks}
The AnDi-2 benchmark \cite{andi2data,andi2025} consists of \emph{simulated} two-dimensional trajectories of at most 200 frames with per-frame labels; it is not a measurement; error estimation for such exponents has been treated with Bayesian deep learning \cite{seckler2022}. One track is one dataset: the time-averaged mean-squared displacement of the first $T$ frames at $J=6$, 7, 8 lags ($T=100$, 150, 200), the two coordinates as the two halves, and a block bootstrap over time origins.

On single-state tracks (constant exponent $\alpha$; $n=400$, 351, 278) the false-change rate is at most 1.8\% for every estimator and $T$. Simultaneous coverage of $\alpha$ at $T=100$, 150, 200 is 94.8, 94.3, 89.9\% for E4c; 93.0, 90.9, 86.0\% for E0; 93.2, 87.7, 82.0\% for E6; and 89.0, 85.2, 82.0\% for E1q. Only E4c meets bar (C) beyond the shortest length, and it too fails at $T=200$ (CP 85.8--93.2). The coverage estimates are lower at longer $T$ for every estimator. The three lengths use different sets of tracks ($n=400$, 351, 278), so this pattern does not by itself establish an effect of track length. Two things are also mixed in these figures. The target $\alpha$ is the model exponent of the trajectory, whereas the estimators follow the local slope of a time-averaged displacement at finite lag, so part of the shortfall may be a mismatch of target and not a failure of the uncertainty. And the plan had flagged that the bootstrap ignores the overlap of displacement windows at long lags; we suspect this but did not test it. We conclude only that the benchmark's calibration was not reproduced on these single short tracks.

On switching tracks ($n=79$, 51, 29; median label-implied excursion of the local exponent about 0.5), power is zero for E0, E4c and E6 at every $T$ (CP upper bounds 4.6, 7.0, 11.9\%), and E1q makes one correct-sign declaration in 159 tracks. The sizing rule of Sec.~\ref{sup:sizing} predicted no detection in all twelve estimator--length strata. This agrees with the measurement but does not test the rule where it predicts detection; measured half-widths are within a factor 1.7 of its law (ratios 0.64--1.72). With these four estimators, single tracks of at most 200 frames did not certify a switch of this size.

\subsection{Central England temperature (no verdict)}
The daily HadCET series \cite{hadcet,parker1992} (1772--2026, no missing days) was deseasonalised on 1772--1850 and analysed by second-order detrended fluctuation analysis \cite{peng1994,kantelhardt2001} at 15 scales $s=7$--896 d, separately for 1851--1950 and 1951--2025, with a bootstrap over calendar years. Long-term persistence of daily temperature, with a correlation exponent near 0.7 over more than a decade of scale, is well documented \cite{kb1998,eichner2003}; a superposition of short-memory processes can give similar DFA local slopes over a finite range \cite{maraun2004}, so a slope alone does not establish long memory. The question was the weather--macroweather transition, which Lovejoy and Schertzer \cite{lovejoy2012} place near 10 days. The test range, 5--20 d, and a decision rule were pre-registered from the author's recollection of this literature, before Ref.~\cite{lovejoy2012} was located. One amendment was made before the real-data output was opened: synthetic fractional Gaussian noise with Hurst exponent 0.65 in the same layout showed that the method itself bends by 0.17 between 7 d and about 50 d with no crossover present (dashed curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:cet}), and the margin was moved from 0.10 to 0.25.

What was measured: in both periods E4c declares a decline larger than 0.25; the point slope falls from 1.41 at 7 d to 0.75 at 112 d (1851--1950) and from 1.50 to 0.68 (1951--2025). An earlier version of this paper read these numbers as a verdict in favour of the transition. We withdraw that verdict and draw none, for reasons raised in review that we accept. The bootstrap over calendar years understates dependence beyond a year, while scales up to 896 d are analysed. The check of E4c's coverage in this layout rests on 100 synthetic records from a single null (93.0\%, CP 86.1--97.1), which cannot establish calibration near 95\%. Any restored verdict would need a validation of the bands under multi-year dependence and a defensible way to locate the change. The second leg of the decision rule was met only through an E6 location set (2.6--20.1 d) that is open at the lower edge of its search grid, and in the second period E6's own lack-of-fit alarm fires. The test range and the size of the change came from recollection and not from a source. One methodological point survives because it needs no verdict: the sizing rule of Sec.~\ref{sup:sizing}, extrapolated to $J=15$, predicts half-widths of 0.51 (E4c) and 0.22 (E6) against measured 0.14 and 0.06, a factor 3.6 too wide, so the rule fails on this data layout as well.

\section{Software, independent verification and reproducibility}\label{sup:soft}
The simulations and analysis use Python with NumPy, SciPy (including the HiGHS linear-programming solver, for E7), pandas and Matplotlib; the outer bounds and the exact rational verification use python-flint (Arb and FLINT), the latter with HiGHS called through highspy 1.16.0. Each amendment was committed to version control before the run it governs, so the order of plan and data can be audited. The exact torus and product curves were recomputed independently in SageMath in 120-bit arithmetic (maximum deviation $1.4\times10^{-13}$ in $d$), and the gasket graph and spectrum were rebuilt there from separate code. The parity lemma (if every nonzero entry of a matrix joins vertices of opposite colour in a two-colouring, every diagonal entry of an odd power vanishes, so a non-lazy walk on a bipartite graph has $q(t)=0$ at odd $t$) was proved in the Lean~4 proof assistant with Mathlib; the proof is in the repository and has no unproved steps. As a reproducibility spot check, one small cell from each of the replication, the outer bounds, the fixed-window comparison and the holdout was re-run from the committed code in a fresh environment with pinned versions (Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.5.5, SciPy 1.18.1, pandas 3.1.1, python-flint 0.9.0) and compared with the stored per-dataset results. The last three matched in every non-timing value (2, 36 and 36 rows). In the replication cell (70 rows) every discrete outcome matched, that is every abstention, window length, coverage flag and declaration, 576 values in all, and every estimator except E7 matched to within $10^{-9}$; for E7, 51 continuous values differed by more than $10^{-6}$ between machines (for example a box constant of 3.5176 against 3.5541), with no change in any outcome; Sec.~\ref{sup:env} traces this difference to the walks, and the same cell re-run under NumPy 2.4.6 matched in all 70 rows. One cell per amendment does not show that all cells reproduce, and they do not all: in part of the datasets the two computing environments draw different walks from the same seed (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:bench}), which is why the E7 counts of Amendment 8 and of Amendment 10b differ (Sec.~\ref{sup:e7}). None of these checks bears on whether the benchmark asks the right question or the estimators are implemented as their authors intended.

\emph{Archive.} The archive holds the code, the pre-registered plans with their commit history (\texttt{PLAN.md}, \texttt{audit/AUDIT\_PLAN.md}), the result tables and gate reports, per-dataset outputs with their checksums, a list tying each number in the paper to its source file, and the SageMath scripts. It is not yet public: it is available to referees on request, with public deposit at publication. Its entry point is one script, \texttt{archive/regenerate\_all.py}. It checks the interpreter against the pinned versions and the checksums of the 72 per-dataset input files, runs the 19 analysis steps (the first run, Amendments 1, 3--9, 10a, 10b, 10c, 11 and 12, and the third-round tables) in a copy of the tree, compares every regenerated table with the committed one and collects the gate verdicts. In its last run all 61 regenerated tables were byte-identical to the committed ones. The pinned environment (\texttt{archive/requirements.lock}) is Python 3.12 with NumPy 2.5.5, SciPy 1.18.1, pandas 3.1.1, python-flint 0.9.0 and highspy 1.16.0. Three limits apply. The script regenerates tables and verdicts from stored per-dataset results and does not re-simulate. The results of Amendments 5--9(b) were simulated under NumPy 2.4.6 and SciPy 1.17.1, so a re-simulation under the pinned versions, or on other hardware, gives a different realisation for part of the datasets (Sec.~\ref{M-sec:bench}); floating-point ties for E6 and the continuous band limits for E7 also differ between machines. And the causal-set runs and the audit are outside the script.

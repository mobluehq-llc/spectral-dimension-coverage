\paragraph*{Constructions.}
All fits are in $u=\ln t$ with $y=\ln\hat P$. Local-polynomial fits use a Gaussian kernel in $u$ multiplied by the return counts as weights, with no boundary correction beyond the polynomial order; the bandwidth is chosen by leave-one-out cross-validation on half A, from $\{0.5,0.7,1,1.4,2\}$ for local-linear and $\{0.7,1,1.4,2,2.8,4\}$ for local-quadratic fits. Half A fixes the window, the bandwidth and (for \textsf{BiasAware-CV} and \textsf{BiasAware-Rule}) the bound $M$; half B supplies the estimate and its bootstrap. The bootstrap resamples the 200 walker blocks ($B=200$ draws; 1000 for the \textsf{MonoLP} box); the split arms resample within the 100-block half used for inference. It re-runs the estimator on each draw, except that \textsf{BiasAware} holds the fitted weights fixed across the draws used to calibrate the band. The change band is built as in the main text for the contrasts $\Delta_j=d(t_j)-d(t_0)$, $j\ge1$, with its own maximum over $j$, so the search over tested times is inside the calibration. For \textsf{BiasAware} the weights $a^{(j)}$ at each window point minimise $1.96\sqrt{a^{\top}Sa}+(M/6)\sum_i|a_i||u_i-u_j|^3$ subject to annihilating constants and quadratics and reproducing the slope, with $S$ the bootstrap covariance of $y$ on half B; $M$ bounds $|d^3\ln q/du^3|$ on the window range; the band is $\hat\beta_j\pm(c\,\mathrm{sd}_j+B_j)$ with $B_j$ the worst-case bias term, and the change band uses $B_j+B_0$. \textsf{BiasAware-CV} takes $M$ as the largest $|6\times\text{cubic coefficient}|$ of a local-quartic fit on half A over the window points; \textsf{BiasAware-Rule} takes the largest third derivative of one weighted quartic fitted to the whole window.

\paragraph*{Zero-count floor.}
Every bootstrap-based arm floors each replicate of the return fraction at $0.5/W$ ($W$ the walkers in the half-sample) before the logarithm. For the split-sample arms with adaptive windows the 30-return rule is applied to half A, and it does not exclude a zero count at a window time in a block resample of half B, so the floor can act; its frequency was not measured. \textsf{MonoLP} does not use $\ln P^*$; its box treats a zero count through a Clopper--Pearson limit.

\paragraph*{Truth and simulation law.}
The exact curves were recomputed independently in SageMath in 120-bit arithmetic (maximum deviation $1.4\times10^{-13}$ in $d$). Each walker is simulated as a single path and its return indicator is recorded at each grid time, so counts at different times are correlated as for the real walk. On the ring $C_{128}$ the exact covariance of one walker's return indicators at $t=24$ and 34 is $q(24)[q(10)-q(34)]=0.009142$; the simulation gave $0.009132$ (standard error $0.000013$).

\paragraph*{Computing environments.}\label{sup:env}
The exploratory comparison, the frozen replication and the first \textsf{MonoLP} run were simulated under NumPy 2.4.6; the outer bounds, the fixed-window run, the holdout, the regression arms, Amendment 13 and the checks of Secs.~\ref{sup:nodrop} and~\ref{sup:redteam} under the pinned NumPy 2.5.5 and SciPy 1.18.1. The two versions draw different binomial counts from the same seed in part of the datasets: of the 1030 seeds regenerated for the outer bounds, the window length equals that of the first environment in 752. Results from the two environments on the same seeds are therefore two Monte Carlo realisations.

\section{\textsf{MonoLP}: box, outer relaxation and verification}\label{sup:e7}
\paragraph*{Box and programme.}
\textsf{MonoLP} forms a simultaneous box for $q$ at the grid times and reports, at each window time, the smallest and largest $d(t)$ over all such measures consistent with the box. The box was designed at 97.5\% and is coded more conservatively. The bootstrap part (1000 draws) takes the 0.99375 quantile of the largest absolute studentised deviation over the times with at least 30 returns, a nominal error of 0.625\%. The remaining times share 1.25\% equally, each with a two-sided CP interval. The nominal total is 1.875\%. The set of times that receive a CP interval is read from the same data and the Bonferroni division treats it as fixed; the bootstrap part is nominal and not exact. The bootstrap critical value is about 3.5 standard deviations where a 95\% max-$t$ value would be about 3.0; we did not rerun \textsf{MonoLP} at 95\%. Each endpoint is a linear-fractional programme, solved as a linear programme (Charnes--Cooper) on a measure discretised to 400 atoms log-spaced in $s$ plus atoms at $s=0$ and $s=\infty$; the atoms are doubled until no endpoint moves by more than 0.005. The grid stops at $s=1$ and then jumps to $s=\infty$; mass at $s>1$ changes the data by at most $e^{-24}$ at $t\ge24$, and 60 extra atoms on $(1,30]$ changed no decision in 1280 paired datasets (Sec.~\ref{sup:redteam}). The band is scored at the sampled window times, a dataset with fewer than two gets no result, and the programme uses all grid times as constraints. No band is returned, and a miss counted, when the solver fails: all 66 such datasets were HiGHS status 4 (numerical difficulty), none infeasible. Of 9400 datasets at 32 walkers per node 66 have no band; all 44 misses on realised crossovers and four of the five on controls are of this kind. The joint test \textsf{MonoLP-Joint} takes the extremes of $d(t_j)-d(t_0)$ over the same set by branch and bound with tolerance 0.002 (distinct from the ball-arithmetic check of Sec.~\ref{sup:nodrop}).

\paragraph*{Outer relaxation (CORRECTIVE REANALYSIS).}
The support of the measure, $s\in[0,\infty]$, is partitioned into the atom at $s=0$, 1597 geometric cells that tile $(0,1]$, the first of which reaches down to $s=0$, and a tail cell $[1,\infty]$. For mass $m_k$ on cell $k$, $q(t_j)$ and the numerator of $d(t_j)$ are enclosed between the sums of $m_k$ times the minimum and the maximum over the cell of $e^{-st_j}$ and of $se^{-st_j}$. The tail is enclosed in the same way and is not truncated: it adds at most $e^{-t_j}$ ($4\times10^{-11}$ at $t=24$) to the upper enclosure of $q$ and nothing to the lower. The constants are computed in 80-bit ball arithmetic and rounded outward, and the box constraints are relaxed separately at each time, so the feasible set contains every admissible continuous measure. The upper endpoint is the smaller of two linear programmes and the lower endpoint the larger. The joint test was implemented for relaxation B only, so its three powers are B-only results. The reanalysis took 1030 dataset identifiers chosen beforehand (the 170 atom-sensitive ones, one with a withheld joint decision, a seeded sample of 300 stable ones, and all 600 of the 3D cells $L=160$, 192 and 256) and regenerated them in the second environment. The outer band contains the exact curve in 1029 and does not exist in one; the discretised bands of the same realisations covered 1024 and did not exist in six, and the outer band contains the discretised one in all 1024. Control coverage is 112/112 (CP 96.8--100.0) with no false change (0/112, CP 0.0--3.2), and crossover coverage 760/761 (99.3--100.0), under both relaxations. The outer-bound joint test declares the 3D drop in 170 of 200 datasets at $L=160$ (85.0\%, CP 79.3--89.6), 186 at $L=192$ (93.0\%, 88.5--96.1) and 198 at $L=256$ (99.0\%, 96.4--99.9). At $L=160$ the bar of 0.8 passes on the point estimate only. The other 8370 datasets were not recomputed.

\paragraph*{Exact verification.}\label{sup:lp}
The programmes are solved by HiGHS in double precision, which leaves the solves themselves unverified. To check them, 44 of the 1030 datasets were selected by a rule and a seed committed before any was solved, in seven disjoint groups (each dataset carries one selection tag): all seven with a non-zero solver status in any column, eight random ones from each of the three 3D minimum-size cells, eight atom-sensitive ones, four stable ones and the one with the withheld decision. For each, every endpoint programme of relaxation B was rebuilt, 2016 in all, and solved by dual simplex, and the final basis was checked in exact rational arithmetic, with each double read as the rational number it is: primal feasibility of the basic solution and the sign of every reduced cost. All 2016 were verified to be optimal in this way: 1646 at the solver's own basis, and 370 after exact rational pivots from a basis that was dual infeasible by less than the solver's tolerance of $10^{-7}$. For the one dataset without an outer band, the emptiness of the relaxed set was certified by a strictly positive exact optimum of a phase-one programme. For the joint test we compared the decisions with this error. A drop is declared when the upper bound on the change lies below $-0.25$. Over all 570 declared drops in the 1030 datasets (554 in the three 3D cells of 200 and 16 elsewhere) the smallest margin is $2.4\times10^{-5}$; two are within $10^{-4}$ and eight within $10^{-3}$ of the threshold, against a solver error of about $4\times10^{-7}$ in absolute terms. The nearest undeclared bound is $2.7\times10^{-3}$ on the other side. Unverified: the other 986 datasets; relaxation A; and the 370\,259 programmes of the \textsf{MonoLP-Joint} branch and bound, which are covered by the margin analysis and not one by one. No one-sided certificate was produced for the joint-test declarations. We therefore describe the outer-bound results as verified on a sample and otherwise numerical.

\section{The global no-drop test}\label{sup:nodrop}
EXPLORATORY. The test, its grid, search, solver, outcomes, bars and predictions were registered in a file committed before any registered cell was run (\texttt{PREREG\_GLOBAL\_NODROP.md}); only a synthetic unit test of the solver preceded it. The minimum drop, the continuum certificate and its two ball rechecks (P8, P9) were registered in amendments of \texttt{PREREG\_REDTEAM\_FINAL.md} before their runs. No frozen file was edited.

\paragraph*{Null and admissible set.}
The admissible set is that of \textsf{MonoLP} and \textsf{MonoLP-Joint}: $q(t)=\sum_k w_k e^{-ts_k}$ with $w\ge0$, $\sum_k w_k=1$, mass at least $1/N$ at $s=0$, and $q$ inside the box at every grid time. With $a(t)=\sum_k w_k s_k e^{-ts_k}$, $d(t)=2t\,a(t)/q(t)$. The \emph{global no-drop null} is that some admissible measure has $d(t_j)\ge d(t_0)-0.25$ at every later window time $t_j$. The joint test declares a drop only if a single $t_j$ drops for all measures; under the global test the dropping time may depend on the measure, so for the ideal tests on one feasible set at one margin the global test declares whenever the joint test does. The implementation has a guard of 0.005 on the margin and can return INCONCLUSIVE, so this is a property of the ideal tests, checked on the data below.

\paragraph*{Search.}
For a fixed value $r$ of $d(t_0)$ the constraints $2t_0a(t_0)=r\,q(t_0)$ and $2t_ja(t_j)\ge(r-0.25)\,q(t_j)$ are linear in $w$, so the null is a one-parameter family of feasibility programmes. The range of $r$ is the \textsf{MonoLP} band at $t_0$, split into six intervals and refined by branch and bound (bisection, smallest interval $2\times10^{-4}$, at most 400 programmes per atom grid). On an interval $[r_a,r_b]$ an \emph{outer} programme imposes $d(t_0)\in[r_a,r_b]$ and $d(t_j)\ge r_a-0.25-0.005$ for all $j$; if it is infeasible, no admissible measure with $d(t_0)$ in that interval avoids a drop larger than 0.255. An \emph{inner} programme imposes $d(t_j)\ge r_b-0.25$; a measure that it returns is checked independently of the solver (weights on the simplex to $10^{-6}$, atom at zero, box to a relative $10^{-7}$, and the smallest change recomputed from the measure). The programmes are solved by HiGHS in double precision on 800 atoms.

\paragraph*{Outcomes.}
DROP: every interval is excluded and none is unresolved, on the 800-atom grid and again on a 1597-atom grid. WITNESS: a verified no-drop measure is returned. INCONCLUSIVE: an unresolved interval below the smallest width, the limit of 400 programmes, or a numerical solver status; a drop on the coarse grid that the fine grid does not repeat is also inconclusive. A box that no measure fits is counted separately and never as a drop; it did not occur. For each dataset a verified measure \emph{with} a drop larger than 0.25 was also sought; where both witnesses exist the box cannot decide.

\paragraph*{Exact returns (sign test).}
The registration required DROP on $C_{768}\times C_{16}$ with exact returns before any data run could be reported, because log-convexity secants through the exact returns at $t=24$, 34, 4352 and 6144 already give $d(24)\ge1.6744$ and $d(6144)\le1.1824$, a drop of at least 0.4920. With the box set to the exact $q$ times $1\pm\epsilon$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$, the outcome was DROP on $C_{768}\times C_{16}$ (exact largest drop in the window 1.004) and on $C_{160}\times C_8^2$ (2.020), and WITNESS on the ring $L=1024$, the 2D torus $L=96$ and the 3D torus $L=24$ (exact largest drops 0.000, 0.004 and 0.053), at all three widths. Narrower boxes cannot be met on the 800-atom grid.

\paragraph*{Minimum compatible drop.}
The mathematical target is $D^\ast=\inf_\nu\max_{j\ge0}[d_\nu(t_0)-d_\nu(t_j)]\ge0$ over admissible measures $\nu$ (the $j=0$ term is zero, so $D^\ast\ge0$). With the margin 0.25 replaced by $\delta$, $D_{\rm lo}$, the lower endpoint returned by the two-grid search, is the largest $\delta$ at which the test above (both grids, tolerance 0.005 on the margin) returns DROP, found by bisection on $[0,\,\max d(t_0)]$ to 0.005. Restricting the feasible measures to a grid can raise an infimum, so $D_{\rm lo}$ has no guaranteed direction of error against $D^\ast$, and agreement on two grids does not settle it; bar B4 below compares it with the exact drop of the generating graph, not with $D^\ast$, and the data are not read as establishing any fraction of the true drop; $\delta=0$ is tried first, and if it is not DROP then $D_{\rm lo}=0$. For the $D_{\rm lo}$ search, a DROP at $\delta$ excludes no-drop measures on the two tested atom grids, subject to solver tolerances: no gridded admissible measure avoids a drop of more than $\delta+0.005$ at every window time. $D_{\rm lo}$ is quoted without the 0.005. An INCONCLUSIVE step counts as not DROP; every bisection met at least one next to its boundary, so only the lower end of each bracket is clean. The bisection never tests $\delta=0.25$, so agreement with the stored declarations is a check. Four bars were registered. B1, exact returns: $D_{\rm lo}$ within $[0.482,1.004]$ in 2D (the secant certificate less 0.01, and the exact drop) and at most 2.020 in 3D; observed 0.924 and 0.718 (2D, $\epsilon=10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$) and 1.900 and 1.632 (3D). B2, consistency: no dataset has $D_{\rm lo}\ge0.25$ with a stored outcome other than DROP; of the 37 stored 2D declarations 33 have $D_{\rm lo}>0.25$ and four have $D_{\rm lo}=0.2465$--0.2496, inside the tolerance. B3, controls: $D_{\rm lo}=0$ in the first 30 datasets of each control cell (90 of 90) and in the six exact-return control boxes. B4, truth: $D_{\rm lo}$ at most the exact drop in 100 of 100 datasets in each cell (bar 95; CP lower limit 96.4\%). All four passed. At 32 walkers per node the minimum, quartiles and maximum of $D_{\rm lo}$ are 0.045, 0.183, 0.224, 0.255 and 0.477 in 2D, with 33 of 100 above 0.25 (CP 23.9--43.1) and none above 0.5; and 0.287, 0.587, 0.681, 0.769 and 1.107 in 3D, with 100 above 0.25 and 87 above 0.5 (CP 78.8--92.9). Of the seven predictions recorded in advance two failed: that the 2D count above 0.25 would be within three of 37 (it is 33) and that the 2D median would be at most 0.2 (0.224).

\paragraph*{Continuum certificate of the 37 declarations.}
For each interval of $r$ left by the search on the 800-atom grid, and for four pad intervals that extend the range of $r$ below and above the grid band so that measures off the grid are covered, an elastic phase-one programme gives multipliers whose dual function is $g(s)=v_1+\sum_j(P_j+Q_js)e^{-t_js}$. The interval is \emph{certified} if, after a shift of the multipliers, $g\ge0$ on the whole support with a strictly negative objective: on $(0,1]$ by interval branch and bound with a second-order centred enclosure, on $s\ge1$ by the bound $(|P_j|+|Q_j|)e^{-t_j}$, and at the atoms $s=0$ (with its $1/N$ floor) and $s=\infty$. A point where the check fails is added as an atom and the programme solved again (at most 12 rounds). Certified, not certified and solver failure were to be counted separately. Result: 37 certified, 0 not certified, 0 solver failures, over 485 intervals (10 to 29 per dataset), the 148 pad intervals among them. Sixteen datasets needed added atoms (619 in all, at most 148 in one); 21 certified on the grid alone. In the first run ten declarations, drawn by a seeded rule fixed in advance, were re-verified interval by interval in 100-bit Arb ball arithmetic, and the other 27 rested on floating-point evaluation inflated by a relative $10^{-12}$, without directed rounding. A recheck registered afterwards (P8) applied the ball check to all 37 in the pinned environment (\texttt{results/certify\_2d\_mpall.csv}): all 37 were certified and every certificate passed the ball check; the regenerated verdict, cell count (485) and added atoms (619) matched the first run for every dataset. The ball check encloses the dual function, with the double-precision multipliers read as exact numbers, over the whole support; the multipliers themselves come from the floating-point LP. \emph{Coverage of $r$.} The cells tile the real line. The leaves of the $r$-bisection share endpoints and tile $[a_0,b_0]$, with $a_0$ and $b_0$ the extremes of $d(t_0)$ on the 800-atom grid, less and plus $10^{-9}$. The pad cells $[a_0-0.005,a_0]$ and $[b_0,b_0+0.005]$ are ordinary cells. The two end cells, $(-\infty,a_0-0.005]$ (the box with $d(t_0)\le a_0-0.005$ and no further row) and $[b_0+0.005,\infty)$ (the box with $d(t_0)\ge b_0+0.005$ and the no-drop rows), are certified in the same way. Every constraint $r_a\le d_\nu(t_0)\le r_b$ is linear in $\nu$ and holds for continuum measures, so every admissible value of $r=d_\nu(t_0)$ lies in a certified cell. Exact equality of the shared endpoints was asserted for all 37 datasets (P9). \emph{Ball check of the objective.} The first check evaluated the objective $c-\tau_s$ (with $c$ the constant of $g$ plus its shift and $\tau_s$ the right-hand side) from doubles. A further check (P9, registered before the run, \texttt{rt\_certify\_arbfull.py}) recomputed $P_j$, $Q_j$, $\tau_s$ and $c$ in 100-bit balls from the raw multipliers, the box and the cell thresholds, and tested $g\ge0$ on the whole support and the strictly negative objective in ball arithmetic: all 485 cells of the 37 datasets passed, with the largest upper bound of the objective $-2.2\times10^{-5}$, the smallest ratio of the negative objective to the phase-one value 0.90, and cell and atom counts equal to the first run. The falsifiers behaved as registered: a damaged constant or multiplier fails, and a gap or a missing leaf breaks the tiling. Sentinels, all passed: the exact-return box certifies; around the stored witness of each of the 58 WITNESS datasets, and on 10 ring datasets, a feasible measure is returned and nothing certifies; with exact returns a margin above the true drop gives a witness and one below it certifies; a deliberately damaged certificate fails the ball check. The certificate concerns the decision given the stored box. It says nothing about the coverage of that box, which is nominal, and it was not applied to the 3D declarations or to $D_{\rm lo}$, which rest on finite atom grids.

\paragraph*{Limits.}
The drop is a finite-graph result: $d=[1-1/(Nq)]\,d_{\rm excess}$ where $d_{\rm excess}=-2\,d\ln(q-1/N)/d\ln t$, so equilibration alone depresses $d$ (factor 5/6 at $Nq=6$); these drops are larger. The $D_{\rm lo}$ values and the 3D declarations are established to the floating-point tolerance of the solver, with a margin of 0.005 and on finite atom grids (800 and 1597). The exact rational check of Sec.~\ref{sup:e7} was not applied to this test. There is one cell per family, 100 datasets each, at 32 walkers per node; other budgets and sizes were not run, and a larger programme limit was not tried for the five inconclusive datasets. The endpoint test declared no drops larger than 0.25 in the 100 two-dimensional datasets.

\section{Bias-aware bands: diagnoses and the split-covariance test}\label{sup:e4}
\paragraph*{Fitted bound and allowance (ungated).}
At fixed windows the fitted bound $M$ is below the fine-grid reference maximum in 17.3--43.7\% of datasets for \BAC{} and 25.3--53.0\% for \BAR{}; coverage is 87--94\% among those datasets and 99--100\% among the others. With the oracle bound the realised bias never exceeded half of the allowance. Together with the optimistic noise calibration of the oracle arm below, this is why the gated coverage is consistent with partially cancelling errors; it is an inference, not a measured decomposition.

\paragraph*{Oracle arm (EXPLORATORY).}
No gate; specification committed before the run. The oracle arm \textsf{BiasAware-Oracle}, whose $M$ is a fine-grid maximum computed from the exact spectrum (600 log-spaced points plus the observation times, with a $10^{-3}$ floor), failed the cell rule on the ring $L=512$ in the exploratory comparison. We re-executed the frozen \textsf{BiasAware-Oracle} code on the four ring cells with $M$ fixed at that value, and kept for every window point the exact smoothing bias $b_j$ of the weights, the allowance $B_j$, the stochastic part $n_j$ of the estimate, its bootstrap standard deviation and the critical value $c$. On the original seeds the rerun reproduced every stored abstention, window length, half-width and coverage flag, a check stated in advance. By a rule also stated in advance, a miss is a \emph{bound} miss if $|b_j|>B_j$ at some window point and a \emph{calibration} miss otherwise; in the second case the stochastic part must have left its bootstrap band. A second arm used 4000 fresh seeds per cell.

The result (per-ring values are in the archive): No miss is a bound miss. In the 15\,125 answered datasets the exact bias never exceeded the allowance, and the largest ratio is 0.50. All 62 misses on the original datasets and all 1110 on fresh seeds are calibration misses. With 4000 datasets per cell the complete band of the oracle arm, bias allowance included, covers 92.0--92.6\% in every ring cell, so the shortfall is general, and the 88.5\% of the one failing cell (CP 83.2--92.6) is consistent with a level near 92\% together with sampling error. The stochastic part alone stays inside its bootstrap band in 84--92\% of datasets. The median bootstrap standard deviation is 83--95\% of the empirical standard deviation of the same quantity across datasets (smallest value 0.77). The oracle arm is therefore not a 95\% reference on these cells.

\paragraph*{In-sample covariance (EXPLORATORY).}
Specification committed before the run; no gate. In \textsf{BiasAware} the weights are optimised against the covariance $S$ of the 200 bootstrap draws of half B, and the critical value is calibrated on the same draws. On the ring $L=512$ at 32 walkers per node with $M$ at the oracle value, on 1000 fresh seeds, coverage was 91.1\% (CP 89.2--92.8) for this baseline, 93.2\% (91.5--94.7) with 1000 draws, and 96.0\% (94.6--97.1) with $S$, the weights and the standard deviations taken from the independent half A; the mean critical value rose from 2.49 to 2.97, and the ratio of bootstrap to empirical standard deviation stayed at 0.86. The variant changes the weights, the standard deviations and the critical value together, so the experiment does not isolate one of them. This was a diagnosis on one cell.

\paragraph*{\textsf{BiasAware-Split} (Amendment 13(a); CONFIRMATORY).}\label{sup:a13}
\textsf{BiasAware-Split} computes the covariance $S$, and from it the weights $a^{(j)}$ and the standard deviations, from a 200-draw bootstrap of half A; half B supplies the point estimate and the max-$t$ critical value. The bias allowance is computed from the half-A weights and $M$, using the oracle value or the half-A fit. The bound $M$ is as in the original arm: the oracle value or the held-out fit (the CV form). All four arms were run on the same walkers, on the 14 control and 19 crossover cells of the replication design and on the 6 control and 10 crossover cells of the holdout design, at 8, 32 and 128 walkers per node, 200 datasets per cell, on fresh seeds. The bars were those of \textsf{BiasAware-CV}, applied to each variant in each design: (C) at every budget; (F) at 32 walkers per node; (X) simultaneous coverage on realised crossovers at least 90\% at every budget. A variant is validated in a design if all three pass. Both variants were validated in both designs (Table~\ref{tab:a13a}). Three of four ungated predictions held; the fourth (half-width ratio) is in the stored report.

\paragraph*{Robust bias correction and undersmoothing.}
\textsf{RBC} follows Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell \cite{ccf2018,ccf2022} with the two bandwidths equal: the point estimate is the slope of a local cubic at the bandwidth cross-validated for \textsf{LocQuad} on half A, with the same Gaussian kernel and return-count weights, on half B; the standard error is the bootstrap standard error of that cubic slope, which contains the variance of the bias estimate; the band is the same max-$t$ band on the same bootstrap draws. It is our implementation: the reference software is in another language and no pinned port was available. A unit test checks that the cubic slope recovers cubics exactly. \textsf{LocQuad-Under} is the local quadratic at $0.7h_{\rm cv}$. The bar for ``removes the decline'' was coverage of at least 90\% at 128 walkers per node and a fall of at most five points; the prediction was that \textsf{RBC} would raise coverage everywhere, still decline, and stay below 90\% at 128. The bar was not met and the prediction held: pooled coverage is 83.5\% (CP 80.3--86.4), 66.3\% (62.4--70.1) and 47.5\% (43.4--51.6) for \textsf{RBC}, 57.5\%, 44.5\% and 22.8\% for \textsf{LocQuad-Under}, and 50.0\% (45.9--54.1), 26.5\% (23.0--30.2) and 2.8\% (1.7--4.5) for \textsf{LocQuad}, of 600 datasets each. Mean half-widths are 1.03, 0.52 and 0.28 for \textsf{RBC} against 0.58, 0.30 and 0.17. The ratio of the largest bias to its standard error falls to 0.7, 1.3 and 2.4 with \textsf{RBC}: smaller, and still growing with the budget. \paragraph*{Exact low-order moments in \textsf{MonoLP}.}
A referee proposed adding exact structural constraints. For the lazy walk on a graph without self-loops $q(1)=1/2$, and $q(2)=\mathrm{tr}(S^2)/N$ follows from the graph. Three constraint sets were compared on the same regenerated box at 32 walkers per node: the frozen one (B0), B0 with $q(1)$ (C1), and C1 with $q(2)$ (C2), with the atom grid extended to $s\in(1,30]$. The bars (a rise of 0.20 in joint-test power at 2D $L=768$, or a fall of 15\% in median width over the 2D cells with $L\ge192$) were not met and no decision changed: the joint test declared 0 of 100 at 2D $L=768$ and 24, 65 and 87 of 100 at 3D $L=96$, 128 and 160 under all three sets, and the median paired width ratio to B0 was 0.985 (C1) and 0.965 (C2).

\paragraph*{Prior art for \textsf{MonoLP}.}\label{sup:prior}
We read the full texts of Lawrence \cite{lawrence2024}, Mutzel and Tilloy \cite{mutzel2026} and Lehner \cite{lehner2026}. Lawrence bounds integrals of a positive spectral density from noisy Euclidean correlators by convex optimisation over a Mahalanobis region with a bootstrap threshold at the 99\% level, solved by Lagrange duality. Mutzel and Tilloy replace his dual solver by a hierarchy of semidefinite programmes with rigorous bounds and add an infeasibility certificate that flags implausible data, the analogue of \textsf{MonoLP} returning no band when no measure fits the box. Lehner calibrates the interval with a $\Delta\chi^2$ threshold, separates a jackknife statistical error from a systematic part, and reports coverage tests on correlators, smeared densities and step functions. All three work with linear functionals, an ellipsoidal region and Gaussian errors. \textsf{MonoLP} differs in the target (a ratio of two linear functionals, by the Charnes--Cooper transformation), the region (a box from walker counts with Clopper--Pearson limits), the object (random walks on finite graphs) and the crossover tests built on the same set. Stark's strict bounds \cite{stark1992} are the earlier general construction; we cite them for that role and did not have access to the full text. The skeleton (a data-space region, positivity of the measure, an outer relaxation) is prior art, and we claim the application only; the global no-drop test of Sec.~\ref{sup:nodrop} is a feasibility question on the same set and is covered by the same statement.

\section{Two quieter checks}\label{sup:gk}
\paragraph*{Regression standard errors under shared walkers (CONFIRMATORY).}
A pre-registered benchmark took the weighted log--log point fit of Ref.~\cite{gk2022} on our grid and window and varied only the standard error; nothing here bears on that work in its own setting. With the regression standard error the interval covers the exact value in 1716/1930 (88.9\%, CP 87.4--90.3) of answered control datasets at 32 walkers per node and misses bar (C); with a model-based error (93.9\%) or a walker-block bootstrap (93.6\%) it is met. A plausible reason, not isolated by the run, is that all time points come from the same walkers.

\paragraph*{A parametric step (EXPLORATORY).}
A split-sample logistic step in $\ln t$ (\textsf{StepParam}) covers 99.1\% through the single-step product crossovers and 54.2\% (CP 50.7--57.7) on two-step products $C_L\times C_8\times C_b$, against 95.2\% for \BAC{}: a misspecified parametric form fails when the number of steps changes.

# PRIORART_MONOLP.md: prior art for MonoLP (positivity-constrained LP enclosure of d_s(t) and its changes), 2024-11-24 red-team round, updated 2026-11-24 evening with full-text readings

Labels, relative to MonoLP (data-space confidence box on q(t) = int e^{-st} dnu, nu >= 0, then min/max of the linear-fractional functional d(t) = 2t int s e^{-st} dnu / q(t) by Charnes-Cooper LP, outer relaxation of the discretised measure, joint change test):
- **SAME**: the prior work makes the same construction for the same kind of target.
- **SUBSUMES**: the prior work's method contains MonoLP's method as a special case (MonoLP adds only a target functional, a benchmark or a different noise model).
- **ADJACENT**: related problem or tool; neither contains the other.

"Verified" states what was actually opened. Updated 2026-11-24 evening: the full texts of Lawrence, Mutzel and Tilloy and Lehner (arXiv PDFs) were read; Stark (1992) is paywalled and its full text was NOT accessible, so its label rests on the bibliographic record and abstract and the paper cites it only for the general strict-bounds construction. Items marked "not verified" are from memory or from the red-team reviews only.

**Reading.** MonoLP's data-space-region plus positivity plus outer-relaxation skeleton is not new: Stark (1992) and the 2024-2026 lattice-field-theory work (Lawrence; Mutzel and Tilloy; Lehner; Abbott et al.) contain it for linear functionals. The full-text readings confirm the labels; the three lattice papers use an ellipsoidal (Mahalanobis or delta-chi^2) region under Gaussian errors, MonoLP a box of walker-count limits. What the search did not turn up is the specific target (the logarithmic derivative of a graph return probability and its changes, with a joint change test and a random-walk box), which ChatGPT also found no duplicate of. The defensible novelty statement is therefore about the target and the benchmark, not the method. The paper's wording "adapts" (not "introduces") should be kept for the optimisation, and the three lattice papers (Lawrence; Mutzel and Tilloy; Lehner) and Stark should be cited, and are. A literature search with a search-equipped model was not run to exhaustion (WebSearch is capped per session); the "no duplicate" statement is "none found".

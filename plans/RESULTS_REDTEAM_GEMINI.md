## 1. Verdicts on Gemini's seven claims

**(a) "t ~ 140-77" typo: NOT IN THE PAPER, but the sentence it garbles is misleading.** `paper/main.tex` line 61 and the PDF read "near t ~ 140-770" (the review's string is the text cut at a line break). The real defect is different: on C_L x C_16 the exact d(t) falls from 2.00 at t = 48 to 1.896 at 96, 1.64 at 140, 1.50 at 163, 1.31 at 200, 1.10 at 280 and 1.015 at 400, and is 1.000 at 770; the curve is identical for every L >= 96 because the crossover is set by the short ring (1/s_1 = 52 steps for the lazy C_16 x infinity walk), not by L. So "t ~ 140-770; L = 32-768" suggests a crossover that moves with L; it does not. Gemini's own check (gap 0.038, mixing scale 26) used the non-lazy gap and is wrong for the lazy walk (0.0192, scale 52). Suggested wording: "d_s falls from 2 to 1 between t ~ 100 and 400 (midpoint 163) for every L; L sets how much of the 1D regime the window reaches."

**(b) MonoLP box nominally 98.125%, not 95%: CONFIRMED, and the paper already discloses it in the SM and the main text (Sec. III.G) but headlines "97.5%".** `p22amend8.make_box`: `c = quantile(T, 1 - a_box/4)` with a_box = 0.025 gives 0.99375 for a two-sided max-t, i.e. 0.625% miss; the CP part allocates (a_box/2) = 1.25% over the sparse times; total 1.875%, nominal 98.125% when at least one time has fewer than 30 returns, 99.375% when none does (then the bootstrap part is the whole box). The paper text says "design level 97.5%; nominal total 1.875%" (main Sec. III.G), which is correct but easy to misread. The apples-to-oranges point stands for the comparison of power with 95% bands, and the post-selection of the CP set (count < 30 decides who is Bonferroni'd) is acknowledged in the SM. Effect size: the box nominal level changes the box width by a constant of about 3.5 vs 3.0 sd at the bootstrap part; I did not rerun MonoLP at 95% (not requested).

**(c) Table S7 Outer 198 vs Disc. 194 at L = 256: CONFIRMED as a labelling hazard; not a monotonicity violation of the method.** `supplement.tex` Table e7jsize: Outer (second environment, numpy 2.5.5) 198/200, Disc. same = Disc. all 194/200 (first environment, 2.4.6). The caption says "second environment" for Outer and "first environment" for Disc. only through the phrase "of part 9(a)"; the column header "Disc., same" means "same identifiers", not "same simulated walks". On identical data the outer set contains the discretised set, so outer power cannot exceed discretised power; 198 > 194 therefore measures environment drift, which I can size: regenerating the 3D datasets (L = 96, 128, 160, first 100 each) under numpy 2.5.5 and re-running the frozen discretised joint test reproduces the stored decision in 260 of 300 datasets (86.7%) and the endpoint decision in 283 of 300; in 2D 1280 of 1300 joint decisions agree and the number of "no feasible measure" datasets is 20 regenerated against 11 stored. So seed-identical data are not data-identical across the two numpy versions, in a sizeable fraction of 3D datasets. Fix: rename the column "Disc., same ids (env. 1)", state "different simulated walks" in the caption, and (better) rerun the discretised joint test on the second-environment walks.

**(d) Table S18 column schema: CONFIRMED (shifted columns), but the asterisk is present.** The supplement table (`tab:r6brk2`, rendered S18) has columns Below / At L_min / Above. For the E3 (Collapse3) rows the smallest tested size is itself L_min (128 in 2D, 48 in 3D), so there is no "Below" entry and the L_min entry (with `$^\ast$`) sits in the Below column, the next size in "At L_min", and the last column is empty; the same shift occurs in the rows E1 2D, E1x 2D (and E4r 2D at 32 walkers, whose Above column is empty because the sweep ends). Gemini's "no asterisk" is wrong; its schema complaint is right. Fix: leave the Below cell blank ("-") when L_min is the smallest tested size.

**(e) LocQuad bandwidth floor h >= 0.7: CONFIRMED, and it binds more and more with budget.** Stored fixed-window LocQuad (E1q, `archive/data/raw_amend10c.csv`, 3200 answered datasets per budget): the CV picks the floor 0.7 in 23.7%, 40.0%, 62.9% of datasets at 8, 32, 128 walkers per node, and the grid ceiling 4.0 in 41.0%, 26.8%, 13.9%; mean h falls 2.31, 1.78, 1.27 (my 6-cell subset: 2.49, 2.12, 1.45; floor 21%, 33%, 58%). So at 128 walkers per node the majority of fits sit on the smallest bandwidth the menu allows. A finer grid below 0.7 was not tested; the undersmoothed arm below (h = 0.7 h_cv, which goes below the floor in 74% of cases at 128) is the nearest available test.

**(f) LocQuad-Under results exist in storage and were left out of the tables: CONFIRMED.** `results/raw_amend.csv` holds E1qu (5400 rows, the Amendment 1 arm, local quadratic at h = 0.7 h_cv; 32 walkers per node, original window, original design only). On the 1231 answered datasets of the realised crossover cells its simultaneous coverage is 43.4% against 27.2% for LocQuad (E1q) and 85.7% for LocLin-Under (E1u); it was never run at 8 or 128 walkers per node or at fixed windows. SM Sec. S2 only says the variant was run. New in this round (arm LQU, same 600 datasets per budget as LocQuad): 57.5%, 44.5%, 22.8% at 8, 32, 128 (LocQuad 50.0%, 26.5%, 2.8%): undersmoothing helps and the decline with budget remains.

**(g) Zero bootstrap count in ln P*: handled by a floor, and the floor cannot bind in the window.** Every bootstrap-based arm applies `np.maximum(P*, 0.5 / W_B)` before the log (`p22amend.est_E1b`, `p22honest`, `p22amend5/6`, `p22shape`: floor = half a return among the half-B walkers). For the point estimate the window rule (at least 30 returns in half A and N p >= 6) makes a zero count in a 100-block resample astronomically unlikely (a Poisson mean of at least 15 gives e^-15 or less per resample, about 3e-7, and clustering raises it only modestly). The SM does not state the floor (grep: no hit), which is the disclosure gap. The "studentised error mystery" Gemini attributes to it (S10.B) is therefore not explained by the floor; I did not test Jensen skewness or block-count effects and make no claim about them. MonoLP does not use ln P* at all (its box uses Clopper-Pearson with k = 0 handled exactly, `lo = 0`).

(all exactly computable low moments) were not tried and would behave like q(2). The "biggest lever" hypothesis is falsified on this evidence.

## 3. R2: robust bias-corrected (CCF-style) local polynomial: IMPROVES COVERAGE, DOES NOT REMOVE THE DECLINE

Implementation: no pinned Python port of `nprobust` exists here (it is R), so `rt_rbc.py` implements RBC with rho = 1: local-cubic slope at LocQuad's half-A cross-validated bandwidth on half B, bootstrap SE of that cubic slope (it contains the variance of the bias estimate), same max-t band and same bootstrap resamples as LocQuad. Unit test `rt_test_rbc.py` passes (the cubic slope recovers cubics exactly to 1e-8 for any weights; error rates 3.88 and 15.38 per halving of h against 4 and 16). Regeneration check: the frozen `est_E1b` reproduced the stored coverage, h and half-width on 1800 of 1800 datasets. Cells: 2D L = 128-320, 3D L = 96, 128; 100 datasets per cell and budget.

## 5. R4 (ChatGPT b): the 2D "identifiability limit"

**Verified (noiseless):** on C768 x C16 with the paper's grid, log-convex secants from the four exact returns at t = 24, 34, 4352, 6144 give d(24) >= 1.6744 and d(6144) <= 1.1824, a certified drop of at least 0.4920; t = 6144 lies inside the window (exact window end 8704, 250 expected returns at 32 walkers per node).
**Not carried over to 32 walkers per node (my run, 100 datasets of L = 768):** the same secant construction applied to the box of each dataset (best pair over the grid) certifies a drop of -0.88 at the median and never above -0.43 (the lower bound on d(24) is 1.32 at the median against 1.67 noiseless: the box is too wide); the LP endpoint band (a stronger certificate than secants) certifies a best drop of -0.10 at the median and +0.185 at the maximum, below the 0.25 margin in all 100; the joint test declares 0/100. So at 32 walkers per node the weaker tests are blocked by sampling noise, not only by the test's form. **The decisive test, the global no-drop LP, was not run (planned below), so whether a certified 2D drop exists at 32 walkers is OPEN; ChatGPT's inference "the failure belongs to the joint test, not to the data" is true for exact returns and unsupported at the stated budget.** Adopt the narrow wording: "At 32 walkers per node, the implemented test declared no 2D drop through L = 768" and drop "identifiability limit" until the global test is run; the paper's own "largest drop certifiable is about -0.2" is consistent with the endpoint maximum 0.185 here, but it is a statement about the implemented tests.

**Plan (not run): global no-drop test.** Variables: the same measure (atoms, Charnes-Cooper scaling) and box; for a fixed r = d(t_0), constraints 2 t_0 a_0 = r q_0 and 2 t_j a_j >= (r - 0.25) q_j for every later window time j, with a_j = sum w_k s_k e^{-s_k t_j} and q_j in the box. Feasibility of this LP means one admissible measure has no drop above 0.25 at any time (a no-drop witness); infeasibility for every r in [dmin_0, dmax_0] certifies that every admissible measure drops by more than 0.25 somewhere. Search r by interval bisection with verified bounds (as `sup_delta` does), the exact-rational audit of the final basis as in the SM section "Exact verification of the linear programmes", relaxation B (outer) for the box, and exact q(1), q(2) constraints optional (R1 says they will not matter). Resolution experiment as ChatGPT proposes, on C768 x C16 and C160 x C8^2: exact q first, then fresh walkers at 8, 32, 128 per node, reporting declarations of the current joint test, existence of a no-drop witness, and inconclusive solves against the same box. Cost estimate: one LP per r-interval, about the joint test's 400-500 LPs per dataset.

## 6. R5 (ChatGPT c): claim corrections, each checked against the repository

2. **"No window rule" for MonoLP: false.** `main.tex` line 264 says the band has "no window rule".
